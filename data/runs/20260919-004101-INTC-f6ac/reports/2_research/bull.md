## 3. The Fundamentals Are Inflecting — The Bear Is Fighting the Turning Point

This is where the bear case really falls apart. The bear's fundamental argument boils down to: *"Intel is unprofitable, FCF-negative, and dilutive."* All true — and all **backward-looking**. Let me show you what's actually changing:

**Gross margins are recovering hard.** Q3 2025 gross margin: **38.2%**, up from **27.5% in Q2**. That's a **1,070 basis point sequential jump**. This is the single most important operational data point in the entire report — it tells you the cost structure is healing.

**Capital discipline is real.** 9M 2025 CapEx: **$11,158M**, down from **$18,110M** in 9M 2024 — a **~$7B reduction**. The bear characterizes the -$8.4B TTM FCF as a permanent condition. It's not. It's the **trough of a capex cycle**. When you cut capex by $7B and margins expand by 1,000bps, FCF inflection is a math problem, not a hope.

**The balance sheet was fortified.** Cash + ST investments: **$30.9B**. Net debt improved from $41.1B to **$35.4B**. Equity jumped $8.5B QoQ to **$106.4B**. The bear calls dilution a cost — I call it **$4.4B of new capital stock and $10.4B of minority interest from external parties who did serious diligence and chose to fund this turnaround.**

## 4. Refuting the Bear's Specific Concerns

**Bear: "PE is 855x, earnings are illusory."**
Correct — and irrelevant. The market is not pricing INTC on TTM earnings. It's pricing it on **1.6x book value** (near historical lows) and **3.2x sales** on a **$105B net PP&E base** — over half of total assets. This is an **asset-value + optionality** trade. Nobody buying Intel here thinks they're getting a 855x earnings multiple; they're buying the foundry ramp and the 18A roadmap at a book-value floor. The bear is attacking a valuation framework no bull is using.

**Bear: "Operating income is -$2.4B TTM."**
True, but compare it to the prior 9-month stretch at **-$12,090M**. That's a **~$9.7B improvement in operating income.** The trajectory matters more than the absolute level at a turnaround inflection. And Q3 *normalized* operating income was **+$858M** — positive.

**Bear: "FCF is -$8.4B."**
This is the bear's strongest point, and I'll concede it's the key risk. But note: **operating cash flow TTM ($8,574M) is actually *above* FY2024 ($8,288M)** while CapEx is falling sharply. The gap is closing from both ends. Positive FCF is a **when**, not an **if**, provided foundry execution holds — and the capex guidance reductions signal management sees the finish line.

**Bear: "Dilution — +389M shares in one quarter."**
Real cost, yes. But it bought **$4.4B in capital stock and $10.4B in minority interest** — this is **strategic capital validating the foundry thesis**. If the US government, strategic partners, and institutional investors are putting billions into Intel at these levels, the dilution is the price of survival and scale — and it strengthens the balance sheet rather than loading it with debt.

**Bear: "Goodwill impairment risk — $23.9B."**
The company just demonstrated it can **monetize non-core assets for a $5.5B gain**. Management is actively pruning the portfolio. Goodwill risk is a tail scenario, not a base case, and the market has already priced the stock at 1.6x book — a level that implies skepticism.
