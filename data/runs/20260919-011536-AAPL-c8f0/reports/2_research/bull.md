## The core bull thesis: AAPL is re-accelerating, not maturing into a no-growth mega-cap

The most important data point in this whole debate is not RSI 76. It is this:

- **H1 FY2026 revenue:** $254.94B, up **+16.1% YoY**
- **Q2 FY2026 revenue:** $111.18B, up **+16.6% YoY**
- **Q2 FY2026 net income:** +19.4% YoY
- **Latest quarter gross margin:** **49.3%**, up from 46.5% a year earlier
- **H1 FY2026 operating cash flow:** $82.63B, up **+53% YoY**
- **TTM free cash flow:** **$129.17B**, a 28.6% FCF margin

That is not a company struggling to grow. That is a company that went through a flat patch in FY2023–FY2024 and then inflected hard in FY2025 and especially H1 FY2026. FY2025 EPS was already **$7.46**, up 22.7% YoY. Now the first two quarters of FY2026 show revenue, net income, margins, and cash flow all accelerating together.

So when the bear says “growth durability is unproven,” I’d push back: two consecutive quarters of mid-teens revenue growth, expanding gross margins toward 49%, and operating cash flow up 53% is not one lucky quarter. It is a broad-based operating inflection.

And because AAPL’s capex is only about **2.4% of sales**, a huge amount of that incremental revenue drops down to free cash flow. That is why AAPL can generate $129B in TTM FCF while still investing in the business. That cash engine funds buybacks, dividends, and R&D without stressing the balance sheet.

## Competitive advantages: the moat is still the moat

The bear will say AAPL is just a hardware company with a rich multiple. I disagree. AAPL’s mix is increasingly Services-heavy, and Services carries higher margins. That is exactly what you see in the numbers: gross margin expanded to **49.3%** even while revenue grew double digits.

AAPL’s competitive advantages are not speculative:

- **Ecosystem lock-in:** iPhone, Mac, iPad, Wearables, and Services feed each other. Once customers are inside the ecosystem, switching costs are high.
- **Brand and pricing power:** The margin expansion during a growth acceleration proves AAPL is not just discounting to move units.
- **Fortress balance sheet:** Net debt is only around **$16.2B**, and including non-current marketable securities, AAPL is typically in a net-cash position. Debt/EBITDA is roughly **0.53x**. This is not a leveraged balance sheet.
- **Capital returns:** FY2025 financing cash flow was **−$120.69B**, reflecting enormous buybacks and dividends. Shares outstanding are down to ~14.69B. That buyback machine amplifies EPS growth.

Yes, P/B is 41.4x. But that is a buyback artifact, not a leverage warning. Equity is actually rebuilding: from $73.7B in Sep-2025 to $88.2B in Dec-2025 to **$106.5B** in Mar-2026. The balance sheet is getting stronger, not weaker.

## Let’s take the bear arguments head-on

**Bear point 1: “AAPL is expensive at 36x TTM PE.”**

Yes, 36x TTM PE, 9.8x sales, 27.7x EV/EBITDA, and a 2.93% FCF yield are premium multiples. But the market is not paying 36x for stagnant earnings. It is paying up for a re-accelerating growth story with expanding margins and elite cash generation. If the H1 FY2026 EPS run-rate simply continued, FY2026 EPS could plausibly land around $9, putting the forward multiple in the low-30s. Still premium, but far less extreme. And for a company with 49% gross margins, 32% operating margins, and $129B in FCF, a premium is deserved.

**Bear point 2: “RSI is 76 — it’s overbought.”**

Correct, and I would not chase blindly at $300. But overbought is a condition, not a thesis. In strong trends, RSI can stay above 70 for weeks. There is no clear bearish divergence in the data — price and RSI are both making higher values. The smarter bull response is not “sell because RSI is high.” It is “stay long, add on controlled pullbacks, and manage risk.” The 10 EMA at **$291** and VWMA at **$285** are the natural add zones, not panic levels.

**Bear point 3: “Price is stretched above the 50-day and 200-day averages.”**

True. Price is +12.88% above the 50 SMA and +16.08% above the 200 SMA. That is extended. But that is also what strong momentum trends look like. The 50 SMA and 200 SMA are both rising, not flat. If AAPL consolidates sideways while those averages catch up, that would be a healthy bull-market pause, not a reversal.

**Bear point 4: “The MACD histogram is flattening.”**

It is compressing slightly — from 2.07 to 1.91 to 1.77. But the MACD line is still above the signal line, both are positive, and the histogram is still positive. That is a maturity signal, not a bearish crossover. Until the MACD actually rolls below the signal line, the trend-following evidence still favors the bulls.

**Bear point 5: “Growth durability is unproven.”**

The bear can say that, but the data says otherwise. H1 FY2026 revenue +16.1%, Q2 revenue +16.6%, Q2 net income +19.4%, gross margin at 49.3%, H1 operating cash flow +53% YoY. That is a broad-based acceleration across revenue, earnings, margins, and cash flow. It may not last forever, but right now the burden of proof is on the bear to show deterioration. There is no such evidence in the provided filings.
