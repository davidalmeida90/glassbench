
Conservative Analyst: Let me push back on the aggressive case first, because I think it's confusing a strong trend with a safe entry. The valuation point isn't just a static multiple. For NVDA, a 45.6x trailing P/E, 25.4x sales, and 1.77% free cash flow yield mean the stock is priced for sustained hypergrowth. The report shows annual revenue growth decelerating from 126% to 114% to 65.5%. That's still enormous, but it is deceleration. When a stock trades at 25x sales, deceleration is the enemy. The aggressive analyst says forward P/E compresses to the low-to-mid 20s if the upcoming quarter hits $81.6B and $2.39. But that quarter is flagged as a potential look-ahead, likely not public on 2026-05-15. You cannot underwrite a conservative portfolio on an unverified quarter, and annualizing one quarter in semis is dangerous. The balance sheet is strong, but a strong balance sheet is a solvency cushion, not a valuation floor.

On the trend, I agree NVDA is in an uptrend. Price is above the 10 EMA, 50 SMA, and 200 SMA, the golden cross is in place, MACD is positive, and RSI is above 50. But that does not make the entry safe. The 5/15 action was a 4.4% reversal on 181 million shares, closing near the low, after an overbought spike. That is not automatically a trend break, but it is distribution risk. A close below 218.44 opens 212.67 and 209.82. A break there puts 192.63 in play. I don't need the trend broken to say the risk/reward is unacceptable. I need to know what we lose if we are wrong.
