
Bear Analyst: Bull, you’re making a strong near-term tape and execution case. I’ll give you that: AAPL’s Q3 FY26 numbers were genuinely good, and the trend is up. But you’re confusing a great company with a great entry point. At **$332.27**, the market is already pricing in a lot of the good news — and the risks are not being compensated.

### 1. Valuation leaves no margin of safety

This is the core of my bear case. AAPL trades at:

- **37.6x TTM P/E**
- **29.0x EV/EBITDA**
- **10.4x sales**
- **45.1x book**
- **2.82% FCF yield**
- **~2.66% earnings yield**

Those are premium, all-time-high-region multiples for a company doing **+12.2% TTM revenue growth**. The FCF yield and earnings yield are below the risk-free rate. That means you are paying up for future growth before it is delivered.

You said, “If EPS grows 20%, forward P/E drops to ~31x.” That’s true — but it’s also the bull case. What if EPS grows 10% instead? TTM EPS is **$8.76**. A 10% increase gets you to ~$9.64, which puts the stock at ~**34.5x** forward earnings. Still expensive. A 20% EPS growth target is not conservative; it requires another year of strong revenue growth, stable margins, and continued buybacks. And you’re paying today as if that is the base case.

### 2. Growth is real, but quality is questionable

I’m not disputing Q3: revenue **+16.4%**, EPS **+28.7%**, gross margin **50.06%**. Impressive. But look underneath:

- **R&D is up 24.2% YoY** to $42.90B TTM, with R&D intensity rising from **8.30% to 9.19%**. That’s a margin headwind if revenue growth slows.
- **Tax rate normalized to ~17.9%** from 15.6% in FY25. That removes an EPS tailwind.
- **Capex fell 21%** to $10.04B TTM. In an AI arms race, that’s either incredible efficiency or a warning that Apple is underinvesting relative to competitors. The report flags it as “watch AI capex.”
- **FCF of $136.68B TTM looks huge**, but ~$25B came from working-capital timing. Normalized FCF is closer to **$115–125B**. That’s still a lot of cash, but on a $4.849T market cap, it’s a **2.37–2.58% normalized FCF yield**. Still below risk-free.

So when you say “the yield is low because growth is high,” I say the market has already capitalized that growth. The question is what happens if growth decelerates to high single digits. The multiple compresses fast.

### 3. Two balance-sheet red flags the bull is hand-waving

**Inventory: $11.092B, up 94% YoY and 64% sequentially.** You call it seasonal because Q3 ends June 27. But this is the largest inventory build in the dataset, and it consumed **$4.377B of cash** in the quarter. DSO is flat at ~26 days, which is fine, but inventory is the more forward-looking risk. If the September launch cycle doesn’t sell through, you get discounting, margin pressure, and a cash drag. The Q4 print will resolve it — but at 37.6x earnings, the market isn’t pricing that risk.

**New goodwill/intangibles of $20.342B.** You say it’s digestible. Financially, yes — Apple has $62.4B in cash and only $44.8B net debt. But “digestible” is not the same as “accretive.” This is the single largest unexplained change in the balance sheet, coinciding with a ~$15.7B drop in other non-current assets. Either it’s a large acquisition or a reclassification. If it’s an acquisition, we don’t know the target, the price, or the ROI. If it’s a reclassification, it’s opaque. Unknown risk should be priced at a discount, not ignored.

### 6. What would change my mind?

I’m not saying AAPL is a bad business. It’s a phenomenal business. But at this valuation, the stock is priced for perfection. To turn bearish-to-neutral, I’d need to see:

- A pullback to **$280–$300**, where the FCF yield moves toward 3.3–3.5% and the P/E compresses toward 32–34x.
- Q4 FY26 confirming inventory normalization and gross margin ≥49%.
- Full disclosure on the **$20.3B goodwill/intangibles** item.
- Evidence that the AI/product roadmap justifies the R&D ramp without crushing margins.

Until then, the risk/reward is skewed negative. The downside from multiple compression is larger than the upside from another good quarter, especially with the stock extended and event risk elevated.
