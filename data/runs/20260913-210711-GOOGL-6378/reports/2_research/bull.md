### 3. Financial health: the negative FCF headline is misleading

Let’s tackle the bear’s strongest point head-on: **GOOGL** posted **negative free cash flow of -$5.86B in Q2 FY2026**, and TTM capex hit **~$132B**, about 30% of revenue.

That sounds scary until you look at the balance sheet and cash generation. Operating cash flow TTM is **$185.7B** — a record. Cash plus short-term investments are **$242.5B**. Total debt is **$112.8B**, leaving **~$130B of net cash**. Debt/EBITDA is **0.65x**, and interest coverage is roughly **65x**. This is not a company in distress. This is a company deliberately choosing to invest in the highest-return capex cycle in its history.

Yes, buybacks are halted. Yes, debt tripled in 18 months. But the debt is long-term, cheap relative to earnings power, and the capex is aimed at AI capacity with sub-two-year payback. Once capex growth decelerates, **GOOGL** can resume buybacks and offset SBC dilution. The bear is treating a strategic investment phase as a permanent impairment. That’s a mistake.

For a company growing revenue above 20%, expanding operating margins, and sitting on net cash, that is not expensive. Compare that to peers trading at richer multiples with lower margins. **GOOGL** is arguably the cheapest high-quality AI franchise in mega-cap tech.

The bear will say, “The market is pricing in a lot of monetization.” Correct — but the Cloud backlog, TPU ramp, and AI payback disclosures are exactly the evidence that monetization is happening. The market is not paying for hope; it’s paying for visible backlog and proven ROI.
