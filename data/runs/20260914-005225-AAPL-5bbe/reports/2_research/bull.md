
Bull Analyst: **Bull case for AAPL: This is not a broken growth story — it is a re-accelerating cash machine, and the bear case is mostly a timing complaint.**

Let’s start with the facts, because they matter more than the “overbought” headline.

AAPL just posted Q2 FY26 revenue of **$111.18B**, gross profit of **$54.78B**, and a **49.3% gross margin** — the highest in the visible data. Operating margin was **32.3%**, net margin **26.6%**. More importantly, the year-over-year trend is accelerating: H1 FY26 revenue grew **+16.1% YoY**, operating income grew **+19.8%**, and net income grew **+17.3%**. That is not a company “running out of road.” That is a company with pricing power, mix shift, and operating leverage all working at once.

**Bear, here’s the problem with the valuation argument.** Yes, AAPL trades at **36x TTM earnings**, **27.7x EV/EBITDA**, and a **2.93% FCF yield**. That is a premium. But you are paying for premium economics: gross margin has climbed from **43.3% in FY22** to **46.9% in FY25** to **49.3% in Q2 FY26**. That is a multi-year structural mix shift toward Services and premium hardware. The ecosystem — iPhone, Mac, iPad, Watch, AirPods, Services — creates switching costs and pricing power that most hardware companies simply do not have. Apple silicon gives vertical integration. The balance sheet is a fortress: net debt fell from **$62.7B at FY25 to $39.1B** in two quarters, equity rose to **$106.5B**, retained earnings turned positive, and working capital swung positive. TTM free cash flow is roughly **$129B**. That is not a valuation bubble; that is a quality compounder with an enormous cash engine.

**Let me preempt the bear’s likely objections directly.**

**“Overbought and extended above the 50 SMA.”** True. Price is about 13% above the 50 SMA. That is a tactical entry risk, not a thesis killer. The 10 EMA at **$291**, VWMA at **$285**, and Bollinger midline at **$281** are clear add zones. If you short a new-high stock with a rising VWMA and a golden cross, you are fighting the tape. I would rather accumulate weakness than fade strength.

**“Working capital flattered operating cash flow.”** Fair watch item. But even if you haircut the working-capital benefit, the balance sheet still improved dramatically: net debt down **$23.6B** in two quarters, retained earnings positive, working capital positive, and **$68.5B** in cash and short-term investments. The **$16.96B Q2 tax payment** is a cash-timing issue, not a demand problem. AAPL can absorb it without breaking stride.

**“R&D ramp will hurt margins.”** R&D is now **$11.4B/quarter**, about **10.3% of revenue**. But operating income still grew faster than revenue. This is investment in AI, silicon, and Services — exactly what extends the moat. If Apple were cutting R&D to juice margins, I would be worried. It is doing the opposite.

**“Buyback dependence.”** Buybacks are a feature, not a bug. They are funded by **$129B of TTM free cash flow**, not debt. And they are not the only driver: H1 net income grew **17.3%** organically. Shares outstanding have fallen from **15.117B in FY24** to **14.687B** now, which adds an EPS tailwind — but the core business is still growing.

**“Cyclical hardware, tariffs, regulation.”** Real risks, but not new ones. AAPL has the ecosystem, the Services recurring revenue, and the balance sheet to absorb shocks. Any regulatory or tariff resolution is upside optionality. The bear case ultimately asks you to believe that re-accelerating revenue, expanding margins, a deleveraging balance sheet, and a confirmed technical uptrend are all a trap. The data says the opposite.
