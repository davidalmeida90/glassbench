
Bear Analyst: Bull, you’ve made a strong case for Apple the *company*. I’m not here to argue AAPL is a bad business. I’m here to argue that AAPL is a bad *investment at $300*. And on that point, the data is not on your side.

You keep saying the bear case is “mostly a timing complaint.” Exactly. Timing and price *are* the investment case. A great company bought at the wrong multiple can still deliver poor returns. And right now, AAPL is priced for perfection while the technicals are flashing overbought.

### 1. Valuation leaves zero margin of safety

You acknowledge AAPL trades at **36x TTM earnings**, **27.7x EV/EBITDA**, and a **2.93% FCF yield**. Then you call it “premium economics.” Fine. But premium economics do not make a premium multiple safe. At a **$4.41T market cap**, AAPL is already one of the most expensive mega-caps on earth. A 2.93% free-cash-flow yield is thinner than what you can get in many risk-free instruments. You are paying up for growth that must continue flawlessly.

Here’s the asymmetry you’re ignoring: if growth normalizes from H1’s +16% back to Apple’s recent mid-single-digit trend, the multiple compresses. At **30x earnings**, AAPL is roughly **$250** — about **-17%** from $300. At **25x**, it’s closer to **$208** — about **-30%**. The bull case requires continued double-digit growth *and* sustained margin expansion *and* no regulatory blowback *and* a soft consumer backdrop. That’s a lot of “ands.”

### 2. The “re-acceleration” is not as clean as you claim

Yes, H1 FY26 revenue grew **+16.1% YoY**, operating income **+19.8%**, net income **+17.3%**. Impressive. But Q2 FY26 revenue of **$111.18B** was down sequentially from Q1’s **$143.76B**. That’s normal seasonality, but it means the YoY comparison is doing a lot of work. Apple’s core hardware market — iPhone — is mature. Upgrade cycles are long. The Services engine is strong, but it faces antitrust and regulatory pressure globally.

And let’s talk about that cash flow you love. The fundamental report explicitly warns that H1 OCF strength leans on **working-capital timing** — a big receivables release and an accounts-payable drawdown. Those can reverse. Q2 also saw an unusually high **$16.96B tax payment**. Strip out the timing benefits and the **2.93% FCF yield** looks even thinner. You’re hanging your hat on $129B of TTM FCF, but relative to a $4.41T valuation, that’s not a bargain — it’s a premium.

You say “don’t short a new-high stock.” I’m not recommending a short. I’m saying don’t *buy* at $300.

Look at the tape: AAPL closed at **$299.97** on 2026-05-15, with **RSI at 76.02**, price **+12.88% above the 50 SMA** and **+16.07% above the 200 SMA**. It’s riding the upper Bollinger Band at **$304.51**. The MACD histogram has narrowed for three straight sessions — **2.07 → 1.91 → 1.78** — even as price made new highs. The technical report’s own verdict is **HOLD**, not BUY. You say “participate, don’t chase,” then issue a **BUY**. That’s inconsistent. At $300, your upside to the upper band is about **+1.5%**. That is a terrible risk/reward for new money.

### 4. Buybacks are masking, not solving, the growth problem

You call buybacks a feature. I call them financial engineering — especially at 36x earnings. FY25 buybacks were **$90.7B**, shrinking shares from **15.117B** in FY24 to **14.687B** now. That mechanically lifts EPS. But Q2 FY26 buybacks slowed to **$12.288B** from **$24.701B** in Q1. If that pace continues, the EPS tailwind fades. And repurchasing shares at a premium multiple is not obviously value-accretive; it can destroy intrinsic value if the stock is overvalued.

### 5. Competitive and regulatory risks are underpriced

You wave away tariffs, regulation, and competition as “not new.” But at 36x earnings, even old risks become dangerous when they intensify.

- **AI:** Apple is widely viewed as behind Microsoft, Google, and Amazon. That **$11.4B quarterly R&D** ramp — about **10.3% of revenue** — looks more like catch-up spending than a moat-expanding investment. If AI monetization is uncertain, that R&D becomes a margin drag.
- **Services regulation:** App Store fees, default search payments, and DMA rules in Europe threaten the highest-margin part of the business.
- **China/tariffs:** Supply chain and demand exposure remain significant. A tariff escalation or geopolitical flare-up would hit both revenue and margins.
- **Consumer cyclicality:** Hardware is still a huge part of the mix. A weakening consumer can slow upgrades fast.

### 6. Your “fortress balance sheet” argument doesn’t justify the price

Yes, net debt fell to **$39.1B**, equity rose to **$106.5B**, and retained earnings turned positive. That’s real improvement. But it’s also partly working-capital-driven, and it doesn’t make the stock cheap. **P/B is 41.4x**. The balance sheet provides downside cushion, but it does not eliminate valuation risk. A strong balance sheet is not a catalyst; it’s a safety net.
