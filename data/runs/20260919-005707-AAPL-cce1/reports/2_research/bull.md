No. The buyback is a tailwind, not the engine.

And the buyback is funded by real cash: **$51.6B of FCF in one quarter**. Apple isn’t borrowing to buy back stock. It’s using excess cash generation after investing $2.4B in capex. That’s sustainable.

### 5. “Capex is rising. Current ratio is below 1. Liabilities are high.”

- Capex in FY2025 was **$12.7B** on **$416.2B of revenue**. That’s about **3% of revenue**. Yes, it rose 34.6%, likely for AI/datacenter investment. But that is an investment in future growth, not a profit leak. It’s easily absorbed by $111.5B of operating cash flow.
- The current ratio of **0.97** is normal for Apple. The company runs a negative-working-capital model, using supplier terms and commercial paper efficiently. With $45.3B in cash and massive quarterly FCF, liquidity is not a concern.
- Liabilities-to-equity of **3.30x** includes all liabilities, not just debt. More importantly, equity grew **+32.1% YoY** while liabilities grew only **+5.0%**. Apple is deleveraging, not levering up.
