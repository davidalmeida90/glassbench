The conservative analyst also treats a breakout above 276.79 as pure chasing, but a breakout followed by a successful retest is a legitimate way to confirm the next leg. The answer is not to avoid it entirely; it is to size it modestly.

My moderate adjustment is this: keep the SELL bias as a staged trim, not a wholesale exit and not an aggressive short. If the caller holds a full AAPL position, trim about 15-25% now near 271-272 to acknowledge the short-term momentum fade and create dry powder. Use 269 or 267.38 on a closing basis as an alert, then 261 for swing risk, and 255.01 as the strategic line. Size the residual so that a move to 261 or 255 does not damage the portfolio.

On re-engagement, do not initiate new longs at 271.67, and do not chase the first close above 276.79 with size. Either wait for a pullback into 267-270 that holds with a flattening or rising MACD histogram, or wait for a breakout above 276.79 followed by a retest that holds 275-276 on expanding volume with a positive histogram. Add back in small increments, maybe 5-10% at a time, not a full reversal to overweight. That gives you upside if AAPL’s primary uptrend resumes, but it respects the rich valuation, the MACD divergence, and the cash-flow quality question.

So my final balanced view is that the trader’s FINAL SELL proposal is directionally right as a trim into strength, but I would moderate it. Sell part now, sell more into 275-277 if offered, use wider or closing-basis stops, keep a core residual, and only re-engage on confirmation or a better reset. That is the middle path between the aggressive analyst’s tactical breakout-chasing and the conservative analyst’s overly defensive half-exit.
