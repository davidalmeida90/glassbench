That’s just a more tactical version of the same risk-taking. The conservative mandate is to protect assets, minimize volatility, and ensure steady growth. That means we don’t just trim a little and wait for a breakout; we reduce exposure enough that a drawdown won’t damage the firm’s capital, and we demand a much better margin of safety before putting money back to work.

But we should not let the perfect price prevent us from reducing risk immediately. The market doesn’t owe us a bounce.

A conservative approach would either reduce the position size enough that a wider stop at 261 or 255.01 is acceptable, or use a close below 267.38 as the first warning to cut more. Don’t use a tactical stop that’s inside the noise band.

If we re-engage there with size, we’re adding risk at a worse price. A conservative approach would require either a pullback to a much better valuation zone, or a breakout followed by a successful retest, and even then only with a small, risk-defined position. We don’t need to press. We need to survive.

At a 35.9x PE and a 2.45% FCF yield, AAPL is priced for perfection. Buybacks are less accretive at these prices, and they don’t fix cash-flow quality. If growth decelerates even slightly, multiple compression could be severe. The conservative response is to reduce exposure while the price is still near highs, not to hold a residual and hope. And with no sentiment or news report in this run, we have an information vacuum. That’s not a reason to hold full exposure. It’s a reason to reduce until we have more clarity.
