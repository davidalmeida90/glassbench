The conservative analyst treats it as a loaded position. The truth is closer to the middle: it is a position with real upside optionality, but it is also carrying real binary event risk into an unresolved Q3 FY26 print.

The answer to gap risk is not necessarily zero tactical exposure; it is sizing and hedging.

So here is my moderate adjustment to the trader’s Hold. Keep the Hold as the core decision. No new capital at $189.72. That part is correct. But for existing holders, do not treat “maintain current exposure” as one-size-fits-all. Separate a long-term core from tactical trading exposure. The core can be held with the $175 closing-basis hard risk line, because the primary trend and balance sheet justify staying involved as long as the caller can tolerate a gap toward $150.82 in a worst-case scenario. But the tactical position should be sized so that a 20% adverse gap is survivable. If the caller cannot hedge, then reducing tactical exposure into any bounce toward $192–$196 before Q3 FY26 is reasonable. If the caller can hedge, a protective put or collar around the event is a better way to keep upside participation without carrying unhedged binary risk. I do not know the caller’s holdings or cash, so I cannot say “trim 50%” or “hold full size.” I can say: do not let a stop at $175 create a false sense of security. Size for the gap, not just for the stop.
