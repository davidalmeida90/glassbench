
Bear Analyst: **Bear Case: NVDA — Great Company, Wrong Price, Deteriorating Tape**

Bull, I’ll grant you the most important point upfront: NVIDIA is a phenomenal company. But we’re not debating whether NVDA is a phenomenal company. We’re debating whether it’s a good investment at **$189.72** on Nov 14, 2025. And on that question, your own data cuts against you.

You call this a “bull-market reset.” I call it **distribution until proven otherwise**. Let’s go point by point.

### 1. The tape is not just noise

- Price at **$189.72** is below the **10-EMA at $192.11** for three sessions, below the **Bollinger midline at $192.41**, and below the **VWMA at $196.39** by ~3.4%.
- The **MACD bearish crossover** occurred around Nov 6. The MACD line has collapsed from **5.93 on Nov 3 to 1.70**, with the histogram at **-1.32**. You say “still above zero” — but at 1.70, one or two more down days and it crosses zero. That would turn a “fade” into a medium-term breakdown.
- **RSI is 49.42** — neutral, not oversold. There is no technical imperative for a bounce.
- Heavy-volume down days: **Nov 6 volume 223.0M, Nov 7 264.9M on a lower close, Nov 13 207.4M**. That is supply, not accumulation.
- Lower highs: **$206.55 (Oct 29) → $199.46 (Nov 10) → $190.55 (Nov 14)**. The burden is on bulls to reclaim **$196**, not on bears to prove the trend is dead.

### 2. Fundamentals are decelerating and exposed

- Q2 FY26 revenue grew **+55.6% YoY**, but Q1 grew **+69.2% YoY**. That’s a **13.6-point deceleration in one quarter**.
- Q2 QoQ growth was just **+6.1%** on a $46.7B base. You call it “26% annualized,” but that’s annualizing one quarter. The law of large numbers is real, and it’s starting to bind.
- H1 net income grew **+43.6%** while revenue grew **+61.9%** — bottom-line growth lagged top-line.
- Q2 gross margin of **72.4%** is actually below Q3 FY25’s **74.6%**; operating margin of **60.8%** is below Q3 FY25’s **62.3%**. The margin recovery is not a new record.
- **China risk is not solved.** Q1’s COGS spike proves export policy can hit margins at any time. You call it transient; I call it recurring.
- **Concentration risk** is the elephant in the room. Data-center revenue depends on a handful of hyperscale customers. If one slows capex or shifts to custom silicon, NVDA’s growth stalls. The fundamental report explicitly warns financials can’t capture this.

### 3. The moat is not invincible

You call it a platform moat. I call it a very profitable target.

- **72% gross margins** are an invitation for AMD, Intel, and hyperscaler ASICs.
- Google, Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta are all designing custom AI chips to reduce dependence on NVDA and lower costs.
- CUDA is sticky, but open software stacks and PyTorch support are eroding the lock-in over time.
- The “platform moat” doesn’t mean NVDA can grow 55% forever. Competition and customer concentration are structural headwinds — not just noise.

### 4. Valuation leaves no margin for error

You say valuation multiples were withheld, so you won’t pretend to know the P/E. But we can compute it:

- Trailing EPS ≈ **$3.51**
- Price = **$189.72**
- P/E ≈ **54x trailing earnings**

That’s a premium multiple for a semiconductor company with decelerating sequential growth. You say growth is the valuation cushion. But if growth slows from 55% to 30%, the multiple can compress even as earnings grow. That’s the double whammy.

Buybacks at these prices may support EPS mechanically, but they don’t create value if the stock is overvalued. Q1 FY26 financing outflow was **-$15.553B**. That’s capital returned, not capital created.

### 5. The bull’s risk/reward is flawed

You advocate accumulating at **$185–$190**, with a stop below **$175–$178**, target **$206–$211**. Let’s stress-test that.

- Upside to $206–$211 is roughly **9–12%**.
- Risk to your stop from $190 is **6–8%**. With **ATR at 7.71**, that stop is only about **1.5–2 ATR** away. Normal volatility can hit it.
- If **$185** breaks, next supports are **$180.15 → $178.48 → $175.38**. Below $175, the **200-SMA at $150.82** is ~20% lower. Your stop doesn’t cover the tail risk.
- **Q3 FY26 earnings are imminent and binary.** The stock is still up ~85% since May. Expectations haven’t “cooled” to reasonable — they’ve cooled from euphoric to very high. If Q3 guidance is merely in line, NVDA can gap down. A stop-loss may not protect you overnight.
- Your “add above $196” is buying into the resistance cluster after the move. That’s a momentum chase, not a value entry.

### Bottom line

NVDA is a phenomenal company, but the stock is priced for perfection and the tape is flashing distribution. The technicals are deteriorating, fundamentals are decelerating, China and concentration risks remain, competition is mounting, and valuation is stretched.

The burden of proof is on the bulls to reclaim **$196** and deliver a flawless Q3. Until then, I’d avoid new longs and consider underweighting **NVDA**. You’re fighting the tape and the law of large numbers. I’ll take the other side.
