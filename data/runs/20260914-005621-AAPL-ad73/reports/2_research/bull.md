## Growth: This is not mid-single-digit Apple anymore

The bear case usually rests on “Apple is a mature hardware company.” The data says otherwise.

- **Q3 FY26 revenue: $109.42B, +16.4% YoY.** Q2 FY26 was +16.6% YoY. Q1 FY26 was ~+15.7% YoY. That is a material step-up from Apple’s historical mid-single-digit growth.
- **Q3 FY26 diluted EPS: $2.02, +28.7% YoY.** EPS is growing almost twice as fast as revenue — that is operating leverage, mix shift, and buybacks working together.
- **TTM revenue ~$466.8B, TTM net income ~$128.9B, TTM diluted EPS ~$8.72.** That TTM EPS is ~17% above full-year FY2025 EPS of $7.46. The earnings inflection is real.
- **TTM free cash flow ~$136.7B** vs. FY2025’s $98.8B — a **~38% increase**. CapEx is only ~2.2% of revenue, so almost every operating dollar converts into FCF.

That is the scalability story. Apple doesn’t need massive capital intensity to grow. It generates enormous cash, returns most of it to shareholders, and still deleverages.

## Competitive advantages: pricing power is showing up in the margins

The single most important number in the fundamental report is **Q3 FY26 gross margin of 50.1%**, up **+356 bps YoY** from 46.5%. Operating margin was **32.6%**, net margin **27.2%**. When a hardware company expands gross margins that much while growing revenue 16% YoY, it is not losing pricing power. It is flexing it.

That comes from the ecosystem: iPhone, Mac, iPad, Watch, AirPods, and a high-margin Services segment that keeps compounding. Apple’s brand and switching costs remain among the strongest in consumer tech. R&D was **$34.55B in FY2025, 8.3% of revenue**, so the AI/product investment cycle is funded aggressively without straining the balance sheet.

And the balance sheet is de-risking, not deteriorating:

- **Net debt fell to $44.8B** from $62.7B a year earlier — an ~$18B reduction.
- **Stockholders’ equity rose to $107.5B** from $65.8B.
- **Retained earnings turned positive at $11.3B** vs. −$17.6B a year ago.
- Buybacks are running at **$12–25B per quarter** — $25.11B in Q3 FY26 alone — with FY2025 total repurchases of **$90.7B**, fully funded by FCF.

That is a capital-return machine with a fortress-like cash engine.

## Bear counterpoints: let’s take them head-on

**Bear point 1: “Inventory nearly doubled YoY — +87% to $11.09B. That’s a demand warning.”**

Fair to flag, but the context matters. That inventory is only **10.1% of quarterly revenue** and **2.4% of TTM revenue**. Annualized inventory turnover is still around 20x. Meanwhile, accounts receivable rose **+14% YoY** against **+16.4% revenue growth** — that is not channel stuffing. If demand were rolling over, we would see revenue decelerate and gross margin compress. Instead, revenue accelerated, gross margin expanded 356 bps, and FCF jumped 38%. That looks far more like a launch-related build for the next product/AI cycle than unsold stock. It is a monitor item, not a thesis killer.

**Bear point 2: “AAPL is technically extended — price is 3.18% above the 10 EMA and just above the upper Bollinger band.”**

Correct, and that argues for scaling in, not staying out. RSI is 62.84, not 75. The MACD just crossed zero in late August — this is a young trend. In strong uptrends, Apple can ride the upper band. The risk is manageable: 2× ATR from $332.27 is **$316.19**, which coincides almost perfectly with the 50 SMA at $317.83 and the Bollinger middle at $316.63. That is a logical invalidation zone. If AAPL closes below $316–318, I stand aside. Until then, pullbacks toward $322 or $317 are buying opportunities, not reasons to abandon ship.

**Bear point 3: “Valuation is too rich.”**

Using TTM EPS of $8.72, AAPL trades at roughly **38x TTM earnings** at $332.27. That is a premium. But EPS is growing **+28.7% YoY**, which puts a rough PEG near **1.3**. For a company with **50.1% gross margins**, **$136.7B TTM FCF**, a de-risking balance sheet, and a self-funded buyback shrinking the share count ~1.6% per year, that premium is defensible. The market pays up for certainty, cash generation, and accelerating growth — and Apple has all three.

**Bear point 4: “Apple is behind in AI.”**

The data doesn’t support that as a thesis-breaker. R&D is $34.55B annually, and the revenue/margin acceleration suggests the product cycle is already monetizing. Apple’s AI advantage is not about being first with a cloud chatbot; it is about on-device AI, silicon, privacy, and ecosystem integration. That is a different — and potentially stickier — moat.

**Bear point 5: “China and regulatory risk.”**

Those are real overhangs, but they are not in the provided data as new negative catalysts. The social media sentiment and world affairs feeds came back empty, so there is no fresh shock in this dataset. Apple’s balance sheet, FCF, and buyback capacity give it more resilience than most mega-caps to absorb regulatory noise.
