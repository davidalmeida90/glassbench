## 3. The margin story invites competition, not complacency

You argue that because gross margins are expanding, custom ASICs must not be a threat. That’s a lagging indicator. Margins are high today because NVDA has a temporary monopoly on the most critical AI compute. That is exactly the kind of profit pool that attracts capital.

Google has TPUs. Amazon has Trainium. Microsoft has MTIA. Meta is building custom silicon. These are not side projects — they are strategic attempts to reduce dependency on NVDA and improve their own economics. They buy NVDA today because they have to, not because they want to. As those ASICs mature, and as AMD, Broadcom, and open-source software alternatives improve, NVDA’s pricing power will be tested.

You say CUDA is a 20-year moat. It is. But moats erode, especially when your largest customers are also your largest competitors. Hyperscaler concentration is not a bull point — it’s a vulnerability. A small number of buyers control a huge share of revenue, and they have both the incentive and the capital to dual-source.
