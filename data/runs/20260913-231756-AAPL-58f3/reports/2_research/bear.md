## 1. The bull’s valuation math ignores the *quality* of the growth

You say AAPL is cheap because it trades at ~38x TTM EPS and ~35–36x forward EPS, with a PEG near 1.3–1.5. That only works if the growth is durable and the margins are sustainable. I don’t think either assumption holds.

Look at the earnings bridge. FY25 EPS of **$7.46** was flattered by a **15.6% tax rate** and by FY24’s one-time EU State Aid charge. TTM EPS is ~**$8.74**, but the effective tax rate has already normalized to **17.3%**. That’s a headwind, not a tailwind. Meanwhile, gross margin hit **50.06%** — up 680 bps from FY22. You call that “structural.” I call it **peak-cycle margin**. If gross margin normalizes to 47–48%, EPS falls, and a 35–38x multiple contracts violently.

And your PEG argument is fragile. If FY27 revenue growth slows to 8–10% — which is entirely possible after the current comp-flattered inflection — the PEG goes from 1.3–1.5 to **3.5–4.5x**. At that point, AAPL is not a premium compounder; it’s an expensive hardware stock with AI uncertainty. The current quote already discounts high-teens growth and record margins. There’s no margin of safety.

## 3. Inventory is not a footnote — it’s the key catalyst risk

You dismiss the **+87% YoY / +64% QoQ inventory build to $11.09B** as pre-launch channel fill. But the analysis date is **2026-09-11**, right in Apple’s fall iPhone launch window. This is exactly when we need sell-through confirmation, not just channel stuffing.

Inventory days have jumped from ~**10.7 to ~18.5**. If consumer demand disappoints, this becomes a gross-margin write-down risk in Q1 FY27. You argue a 10% write-down is only ~$1.1B, less than 1% of net income. That misses the point. A write-down hits **gross margin**, and AAPL is priced at **38x TTM EPS** on the assumption that 50% gross margin is the new normal. A margin miss doesn’t just cost $1.1B — it compresses the multiple. That’s where the real damage comes from.

Also, this inventory is being financed by suppliers. **Accounts payable is stretched to $64.5B**, and **working capital has collapsed to $0.49B** from $9.47B last quarter. That’s not a strength; it’s a fragile liquidity setup. If suppliers tighten terms or demand slows, cash flow takes a hit.

## 4. R&D and AI: the capex choice cuts both ways

You frame the **+32.3% YoY R&D increase to $11.73B** as an investment cycle. Fine. But R&D is now **10.72% of revenue**, up from 9.43%. Gross margin expanded 357 bps this quarter and offset it. But that’s backward-looking. If revenue growth decelerates below 10%, R&D becomes a **margin drag**, not an investment.

More importantly, look at capex. AAPL’s capex is only **~2.2% of revenue and declining**. You call that “device-centric and partner-leveraged.” I call it a **vulnerability**. Apple is not building the AI infrastructure that hyperscalers are. It’s dependent on partners for critical AI stack components. That means less control, less differentiation, and unproven monetization. In an AI arms race, low capex protects near-term FCF but risks long-term relevance. The market may eventually re-rate that risk.
