## 2. Macro is a direct headwind, not a background risk

You argue rates are a headwind but manageable. I think you’re understating it.

The macro report shows:

You say “if rates stabilize, the multiple can expand.” That’s a big *if*. The data says the bond market is leaning the other way.

## 3. The free cash flow story is deteriorating, and capex is the reason

You praise the revenue acceleration. Fair. FY26 revenue was **$331.84B, +17.8%**, operating income **+20.8%**, and normalized income **+26.4%**. Those are strong numbers.

But the cash flow tells a less flattering story:

- Operating cash flow: **$182.94B**, but helped by a **$14.19B deferred tax benefit**
- CapEx: **$115.95B**, up **79.6%** and now **34.9% of revenue**
- Free cash flow: **$66.99B**, down **6.5%** — the third consecutive annual decline
- D&A: **$38.53B**, up **30.9%**
- Net PPE: **$337.25B**, up **46.8%**

You say FCF will inflect once capex normalizes. But in an AI arms race, who says capex normalizes? Microsoft is spending against Amazon, Google, and others. If it slows spending, it risks losing AI capacity leadership. If it keeps spending, the depreciation wave keeps hitting margins.

And the depreciation wave is already visible. Gross margin compressed **87bps** to **67.95%**. Quarterly gross margin slipped from **69.0% to 67.2%** over FY26. Cost of revenue grew **21.1%**, faster than revenue. That’s the lower-margin AI/cloud mix showing up.

You call the earnings quality concerns nitpicking. I call them material: FY26 net income included **$5.9B of gains on sale of securities**, and Q4 reported EPS of **$4.81** included a **$3.0B gain** — normalized Q4 EPS was closer to **$4.49**. The reported growth is real, but it’s less clean than you present.
