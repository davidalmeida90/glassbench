
Bear Analyst: **Bear rebuttal — AAPL: You’re confusing a great company with a great buy.**

Bull, I’ll grant you the obvious: Apple is a high-quality business. It generates enormous cash, has a powerful ecosystem, and returns billions to shareholders. But none of that proves AAPL is a good investment at **$233.21** on 2025-09-12. My issue isn’t that Apple is broken. My issue is that the stock is priced for a growth acceleration that the data doesn’t show, while the technical setup is deteriorating and the capital-return machine is already outrunning free cash flow.

Let’s debate the actual evidence.

---

## 1. Growth: “flat” is generous — the top line and EPS have gone backwards

You say revenue is “flat.” The data says it’s still below the FY22 peak:

- FY22 revenue: **$394.33B**
- FY23 revenue: **$383.29B**
- FY24 revenue: **$391.04B**

So FY24 revenue is still **~$3.3B below FY22**. That’s not a growth story. That’s a mature hardware company fighting saturation.

Worse, the bottom line has declined despite the buyback machine:

- FY22 net income: **$99.80B**
- FY23 net income: **$96.99B**
- FY24 net income: **$93.74B**
- FY22 diluted EPS: **$6.11**
- FY23 diluted EPS: **$6.13**
- FY24 diluted EPS: **$6.08**

You keep saying buybacks mechanically grow EPS. But in FY24, EPS actually *fell* versus FY23. Buybacks reduced the share count from **16.33B to 15.41B**, yet EPS still declined. That means the underlying earnings deterioration was large enough to overwhelm the buyback. That’s a serious rebuttal to your “hidden EPS growth machine” claim.

Yes, Q3 FY25 gross margin was **46.5%**, and that’s healthy. But Q3 operating margin was **30.0%**, below FY24’s **31.5%**. R&D and SG&A are rising. Margin expansion is not infinite, especially when Services faces regulatory and antitrust pressure that the bull case keeps ignoring.

---

## 2. Cash flow: elite, but the capital-return program is already stretched

You call the balance sheet a fortress. Let’s look closer.

Apple’s FY24 FCF was **$108.81B**. But total capital returned was **$110.18B** — more than it generated. In Q3 FY25, FCF was **$24.41B**, while buybacks plus dividends were **$25.02B**. Again, capital return exceeded free cash flow.

That is not a perpetual compounding machine. That is a company funding shareholder returns partly from its balance sheet and deleveraging capacity. It can do that for a while, but if FCF slows, the buyback tailwind shrinks, and the EPS support fades.

And the balance sheet is not as pristine as you imply:

- Cash + short-term investments: **$55.37B**
- Total debt: **$101.70B**
- Net debt: **$65.43B**

Apple is in net debt, not net cash. It also has negative working capital of **-$18.63B** and negative retained earnings of **-$17.61B**. You call those buyback artifacts — fine — but they still leave a thinner equity cushion. If there’s another tax shock like the FY24 EU State Aid hit, or a regulatory hit to Services, the flexibility narrows.

---

## 3. Competitive weaknesses: the moat is real, but it’s not growing the pie

The ecosystem is sticky. I’ll give you that. But stickiness doesn’t create new growth if the smartphone market is saturated and replacement cycles are lengthening. Apple is still overwhelmingly dependent on iPhone, and the September launch is a binary catalyst, not a guaranteed supercycle.

On AI, you say Apple is “ecosystem-first.” That sounds nice, but the evidence is thin. Apple’s Q3 capex was only **$3.46B**; FY24 capex was **$9.45B**. That’s a capital-light model, but it also means Apple is not building AI infrastructure at the scale of its competitors. R&D at **$8.87B in Q3** is not trivial, but there is no proof yet that Apple Intelligence drives a massive upgrade cycle. Right now, it’s optionality — and optionality is not a revenue line.

Regulatory risk is also underpriced in your bull case. The FY24 tax-rate spike to **24.1%** versus the normal ~15% showed how quickly net income can be hit by legal/tax outcomes. App Store rules and antitrust scrutiny could pressure the high-margin Services narrative you’re relying on.
