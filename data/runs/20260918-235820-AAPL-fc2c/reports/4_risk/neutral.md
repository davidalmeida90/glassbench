The Aggressive Analyst is treating a strong trend as if it automatically makes this a good entry. The Conservative Analyst is treating a rich valuation and resistance zone as if they justify waiting for a perfect setup that may never arrive. The better answer for AAPL is a conditional, staged buy with a clear split between tactical momentum exposure and core accumulation exposure.

Conservative Analyst, your risk list is valid. You are right that AAPL is at a resistance shelf, that the multiple is rich, that macro and sentiment reports are missing, and especially that the trader’s plan has an internal contradiction. The stop at 290.62 and the first add zone at 291.35 are too close. If price closes below 290.62, the tactical tranche is out before the 291 add makes sense. If you add at 291 with a stop at 290.62, you are inviting a whipsaw. That is a real flaw and the trader needs to fix it.

But your alternative is too cautious. In a strong trend, AAPL does not owe you a pullback to 291 or a retest of 304.51. If AAPL closes above 304.51 on above-average volume and keeps running, waiting for a perfect retest could mean missing the move entirely. Trimming into 304.51 to 310 just because RSI is high is also premature while MACD is positive and expanding, there is no bearish divergence, and VWMA confirms volume-backed demand. Unknown sentiment and world affairs reports are a reason to size smaller, not to refuse participation. A neutral risk approach should treat those as tail-risk budget items, not as a veto.

The moderate adjustment is this. Keep the BUY bias for AAPL, but separate the tactical trade from the core investment. For the tactical momentum sleeve, initiate only one-quarter to one-third of intended full size at 299.97, or wait for a close above 304.51 on above-average volume. Use a closing stop below 290.62 for that sleeve. Do not add at 291.35 while that tight stop is active, because that is the stop/add contradiction. Add to the tactical sleeve only if the breakout above 304.51 confirms and holds.

For the core accumulation sleeve, keep it smaller and use a wider structural risk level. If AAPL pulls back into 291, 285, or 281 and stabilizes with MACD still positive, you can add a separate tranche, but size it so that a move toward the 50 SMA at 265.74 or the 200 SMA at 258.43 is survivable. That means the core sleeve cannot be full size just because the tactical stop is 3% away. It has to be sized to the structural floor. If you already hold AAPL, hold the core, trail the tactical stop below 290.62 or the 10 EMA, and trim only on objective deterioration: a bearish MACD rollover, a bearish RSI divergence, or a decisive close below 281.11. Do not trim just because price touches 304.51.

Because portfolio context is not provided, I would not assume a flat book. Express this as a fraction of intended AAPL exposure, not an absolute allocation. If AAPL is already a large portfolio weight, the bar for adding should be higher. If the caller is underweight, a one-quarter to one-third starter is reasonable.

So my adjusted proposal for AAPL is a conditional staged buy, not a full-throated chase. Start with one-quarter to one-third, use 290.62 as a tactical stop only for the momentum tranche, use 281 and 265.74 as structural risk for the core tranche, and add on either a stabilized pullback or a volume-backed breakout, not both blindly. That gives you upside if AAPL continues, respects the resistance and valuation risk, and fixes the stop/add inconsistency. For AAPL, that is the balanced, sustainable path.
