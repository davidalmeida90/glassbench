
Bear Analyst: # Bear Case for INTC — Rebuttal to the Bull

Bull, I'll give you credit: you put together a polished narrative. Revenue ticked up, gross margin ticked up, capex came down. That's a nice three-line story. But you skipped the part where you actually have to pay for this thing. Let me walk through where your argument breaks, because a lot of it is exactly the "rearview mirror" problem you accused me of — except the mirror you're looking at is one quarter and one line on the income statement.

---

## 1. Your valuation defense is a hand-wave, not a math answer

You dismissed 10.2x sales and 90x EV/EBITDA as a "trailing-multiple trap." Fine — then let's run the forward math instead of asserting it.

TTM EBITDA is **$6.185B** against an enterprise value of **$556.9B**. You said the multiple "compresses dramatically" if margin holds and capex falls. Let's test that. Even if you got EBITDA to **3x** current — call it ~$18.5B, which assumes a successful operating turnaround — you're still at **~30x EV/EBITDA**. Semiconductors trade at 15–20x. To justify today's price at a normal multiple, INTC would need something close to **$35–37B of EBITDA — 6x current levels.** That's not an inflection. That's a miracle.

And crucially, the market isn't paying 90x *current* EBITDA — you admitted that yourself. That means **the recovery is already in the price.** There's no cheap optionality left here. You keep calling the setup "asymmetric to the upside." The research report itself says the opposite: **"Risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside on fundamentals alone."** I'll take the analyst's conclusion over your framing.

---

## 2. The "below the gross line" defense is the bear case, not the bull case

This is the heart of it. You said the Q1 FY26 operating loss is "below the gross line," so it's noise. Let's actually do what the report did: **Q1 FY26 gross profit was $5,347M, but operating income was -$3,136M.** That means the drag below the gross line was **$8,483M in a single quarter** — annualizing to roughly **$34B**.

Now, is that "discretionary, front-loaded investment"? No. That bucket is R&D, SG&A, restructuring, and impairments. R&D in this industry is **the cost of staying alive** — you don't get to call it discretionary and also claim a moat built on leading-edge nodes. You can't have it both ways: either Intel is funding the technology roadmap, or it's cutting to find FCF. You're claiming both simultaneously.

And the trajectory is the tell. **FY25 full-year net loss was -$267M — essentially breakeven.** Then **Q1 FY26 alone: -$3,728M.** That's an annualized ~$14.9B net loss rate right after a near-breakeven year. You're calling that "mid-surgery with vitals trending up." The vitals that matter — operating income and net income — went off a cliff.

---

## 3. Your capex lever detonates your own moat story

You love the capex reduction: **$25.75B (2023) → $14.65B (2025) → ~$14.5B run-rate.** Here's the problem: **those are the same dollars that fund 18A/14A.** The entire "Western sovereign foundry" thesis depends on Intel out-spending to catch up on nodes it has historically *missed* — 10nm slipped for years, 7nm was delayed so badly Intel outsourced to TSMC. You cannot simultaneously argue "Intel is building the leading-edge foundry that only a few firms on Earth can match" **and** "Intel is slashing capex to unlock FCF." Cutting capex to ~$14.5B *starves* the foundry roadmap you're selling.

And even after that 43% capex cut, what did we get? **TTM FCF is still -$3,119M.** TTM capex is $13.1B against $9,980M of operating cash flow. **Capex still exceeds OCF.** The "FCF inflection" you keep promising hasn't arrived — and Q1 FY26 needed a **+$3,093M investing cash inflow (asset sales)** to keep the liquidity picture stable. That's not an inflection. That's plugging a hole with disposals.

---

## 4. "Strategic scarcity" is doing a lot of heavy lifting for an unproven story

You called Intel the "only Western-scale IDM alternative to TSMC." Technically true, strategically overstated. **TSMC is building in Arizona. Samsung is building in Taylor, Texas.** The US government's sovereign-capacity goal does not require Intel specifically — it requires *fabs on US soil*, and Intel now has well-funded competitors building them.

Meanwhile, your category-change thesis hinges on **anchor foundry customers** — and the report names **none**. You're asking investors to pay 10x sales for a foundry re-rating that has zero confirmed contracts in the data. That's not a base case; that's a hope.

And zoom out on the actual business: **revenue went from $79B (FY21) to ~$53–54B — a 33% collapse** — and has been flat for three years. Gross margin went from **55.4% (FY21) to 39.4%**. That "670 bps recovery" you're celebrating is a partial climb back from a ~1,600 bps collapse. This is a company that gave away a huge chunk of its pricing power, and one +7.2% quarter doesn't prove it gets it back.

## 5. Your "fortress balance sheet" doesn't protect the stock — it protects the company

Nobody's arguing insolvency. You've built a straw man there. **$32.8B cash, $111.4B equity, ~$10.2B net debt** — great. That means INTC doesn't go to zero. It says nothing about whether **$108.77 is a sane price.**

Here's the number you're avoiding: **book value is $22.16/share.** The stock trades at **4.9x book.** Historically INTC traded at **1–2x book.** Even a generous "successful turnaround" re-rating to 2.5x book gets you to roughly **$55/share — a 50% drawdown** — and that's *with* the turnaround working. The fortress balance sheet isn't your downside protection; it's the reason the floor is at a fraction of today's price.
