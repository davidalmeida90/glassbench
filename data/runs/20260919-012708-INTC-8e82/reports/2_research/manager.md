**Recommendation**: Sell

**Rationale**: The bull's INTC turnaround story has real threads: Q1 FY26 revenue +7.2% YoY, gross margin up to 39.4% from 34.8%, capex down to ~$14.5B, OCF ~$10B TTM, and a fortress balance sheet with $32.8B cash and ~$10.2B net debt. But the bear successfully reframed each point. The below-the-gross-line drag was ~$8.5B in Q1 alone (~$34B annualized), so R&D/SG&A/restructuring are recurring costs of the business, not one-time noise. TTM FCF is still -$3.1B, Q1 needed +$3.1B of investing inflows, and the capex cuts undercut the 18A/14A foundry thesis the bull relies on, with no named anchor customers. FY25's near-breakeven was flattered by a +$4.06B non-operating gain. On valuation, the bull never supplied forward math: at 10.2x sales, ~90x EV/EBITDA, and 4.9x book, even a 3x EBITDA recovery leaves ~30x EV/EBITDA versus 15-20x for semis, and a generous 2.5x book re-rating points to ~$55. The report itself flags downside asymmetry. Technicals are also bearish: RSI divergence at the high, first close below the 10 EMA, MACD histogram near zero, heavy down-volume, and an air pocket to $97.57 then $69.50. The bull's positives are real but already in the price and do not justify the risk; the bear case wins decisively.

**Strategic Actions**: Treat INTC as Sell/avoid. If long, sell into strength rather than at support; use the $111-112 zone (10 EMA) as the first exit window, and do not wait for a close below the $107.78 VWMA. If INTC closes below $107.78, accelerate remaining sales; next downside references are $97.57, then $69.50. If flat, do not initiate and do not buy the dip at $108. For portfolios that must retain semiconductor exposure, hold no more than half a standard allocation in INTC and prefer more reasonably valued names. Only revisit the bearish stance if INTC reclaims $111.65 on volume with the MACD cross avoided and/or confirms named foundry customers or positive FCF; until then, keep exposure at zero or underweight.
