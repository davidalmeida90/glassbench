
Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for AAPL — Opening Statement

Alright, let me lay out why I'm on the long side of AAPL, and let me do it with the data in front of us rather than vibes. The bear hasn't shown up yet, so I'll set the table — and I'll preempt the three or four objections I know are coming, because none of them survive contact with the numbers.

## 1. The growth story is inflecting, not stalling

The single most important line in this entire dataset is this: **AAPL's revenue trajectory has turned.** Look at the arc:

- FY2023: $383.29B (**−2.8%**)
- FY2024: $391.04B (**+2.0%**)
- TTM (to Jun-25): **$408.62B (+4.5% vs. FY2024)**

That's a company that bottomed in FY2023, re-accelerated through FY2024, and is now compounding faster than it has in years. And critically, the *quality* of that revenue is improving, not just the quantity. **Gross margin has expanded ~320 basis points — from 43.3% in FY2022 to 46.5% in Q3 FY2025** — as the mix shifts toward high-margin Services. Operating margin is sitting at a **best-in-class 30.0%**, net margin at **24.9%**.

Any bear who tells you Apple is a "no-growth hardware company" is reading a stale script. The TTM number is the tell: growth is back, and it's higher-margin growth.

## 2. The buyback flywheel is a structural EPS engine

Here's something the bears consistently underweight: **AAPL reduced its diluted share count from 16.33B in FY2022 to 14.95B in Q3 FY2025 — an ~8.5% reduction.** That's why TTM EPS (~$6.62, **+9%**) is growing faster than net income (+6%). Even in a flat-net-income world, the float shrink mechanically lifts per-share value.

And the capacity to keep doing this is enormous: **~$96–109B in annual free cash flow**, funding roughly **$110B/year in capital returns** — a **~3.2% total shareholder yield** (buyback-dominated, far above the 0.45% headline dividend). That is a persistent, structural bid under the stock.

## 3. The balance sheet is a fortress — full stop

Let me put the valuation scarecrow to bed on the risk side:
- **Net debt / EBITDA: ~0.46x** — barely levered
- **Interest coverage: >30x** — fortress-level
- **Total debt down 23% since FY2022** ($132.5B → $101.7B) while the business grew

Yes, the **P/B of 52.8x** looks alarming — but that is an *artifact of buybacks*, not distress. When you shrink equity deliberately, book value collapses and P/B inflates. Anyone using P/B as a bear thesis here is misreading the accounting. Working capital is negative (−$18.6B), but again — that's a **structural feature of Apple's supplier-financed model**, not a liquidity problem, and it's contradicted by **~$27.9B in quarterly operating cash flow**.

So the bear's "momentum is cooling" point is real but *late*. It describes a shakeout that has already mostly happened, into a structural backdrop that is improving.

## 5. Let me knock down the other bear pillars before they're raised

**"35x P/E is too expensive."** Premium multiple, yes — but you're paying for **expanding gross margins (43.3%→46.5%)**, **re-accelerating revenue**, **~$96B FCF**, and **AI optionality** (R&D intensity up from 8.0% to 9.4% of revenue). This isn't 35x on stagnant earnings; it's 35x on a business whose earnings quality and per-share compounding are both inflecting higher. Multiple should be judged *against growth inflection*, and this one is improving.

**"TTM FCF fell to ~$96B from $108.8B."** Correct — and the reason matters: **CapEx rose ~31% (to $12.38B) for AI data-center and infrastructure buildout.** That's *strategic investment*, not deterioration. You don't penalize a company for spending to win the next platform cycle; you penalize it for *not* doing so.

**"China/tariffs and antitrust."** Genuine overhangs — I won't pretend otherwise. But they are well-telegraphed, partially priced, and the company is still posting margin expansion *through* them. Services grows despite DMA/DOJ noise, and the ecosystem lock-in is the deepest in tech.

The fundamental report lands on HOLD — "quality at a full price, add on weakness." I respect the rigor, but I think it's too timid given the inflection. Here's my synthesis: you have a company with **re-accelerating revenue, expanding margins, a fortress balance sheet, and a 3.2% shareholder yield** — trading in a **confirmed intermediate uptrend, above all major moving averages, on the verge of a golden cross, with momentum indicators freshly reset.** That combination doesn't call for a "wait for a pullback" stance. That calls for being long, buying dips toward the $232 (10 EMA/VWMA) and $225 support zones, and adding on the golden-cross confirmation.
