## The growth story is not peaking — it’s re-accelerating

The single most important number in the fundamental report: **Q3 FY2026 revenue was $57.0B, up 62.5% year-over-year and 21.9% quarter-over-quarter.** That is *acceleration* off an already massive base. Net income rose 65.3% YoY to $31.9B, and diluted EPS hit $1.30, up from $1.08 in Q2 and $0.76 in Q1.

For the first nine months of FY2026, revenue was $147.8B, up 62.1% YoY. TTM revenue is $187.1B with net income of $99.2B — a 53% net margin. This is not a company running out of runway. It is a hyper-growth franchise operating at unprecedented scale.

The bear will likely say, “The stock is priced for perfection.” But with ~60% top-line growth, the TTM PE of 44.8x implies a **PEG ratio of roughly 0.7–0.8x**. That is not obviously expensive if growth sustains. And the latest filed quarter says growth is sustaining — actually re-accelerating.

## Competitive advantages: NVDA owns the AI compute stack

NVDA is not just selling GPUs. It owns the full-stack accelerated computing platform: CUDA software, AI networking through NVLink/InfiniBand/Spectrum-X, gaming, professional visualization, automotive, and robotics. The moat is the ecosystem lock-in. Developers, cloud providers, and enterprises build on CUDA because it works and because switching costs are enormous.

The balance sheet is a weapon. NVDA has **~$52.1B in net cash**, debt-to-equity of just **7.1%**, ROE near **100%**, and ROA near **69%**. That lets it out-invest rivals, absorb policy shocks, and keep buying back stock. FY2025 alone saw a **$42.4B financing outflow** — mostly buybacks. This is a company funding its own growth and returning capital aggressively.

Margins prove the pricing power. Gross margin recovered from **60.5% in Q1 FY2026** — hit by a one-time export-control charge — to **72.4% in Q2** and **73.4% in Q3**. Operating margin expanded to **63.2%**. TTM free cash flow is **$77.3B**, a 41.3% FCF margin. CapEx is only 3.1% of revenue. This is an asset-light, high-ROIC machine.

## Countering the bear’s likely arguments

**Bear point 1: Valuation leaves no margin of safety.**  
Fair concern, but incomplete. The FCF yield is 1.74%, which is thin — but that is a function of the multiple, not weak cash generation. Absolute FCF is $77.3B. The PEG is ~0.7–0.8x. If NVDA delivers another quarter anywhere near Q3’s momentum, forward earnings will compress the multiple quickly. Premium quality deserves a premium multiple when growth is 60% and net margins are 53%.

**Bear point 2: Q4 FY2026 earnings is a binary event.**  
True, it’s a risk. But it’s also a catalyst. Q3 showed re-accelerating revenue, recovered margins, and strong EPS leverage. The burden is on the bear to show why that momentum suddenly stops. With ATR at $6.40 — about 3.5% of price — position sizing matters, but the event cuts both ways.

**Bear point 3: China export controls could hit again.**  
The Q1 FY2026 gross margin dip to 60.5% showed the exposure. But look at the recovery: 72.4% then 73.4%. The drag was contained. NVDA has $52B in net cash to absorb policy shocks, and global AI demand — sovereign AI, enterprise AI, cloud — is diversifying away from any single geography.

**Bear point 4: Cash conversion is below 1x.**  
OCF/NI of 0.84x is normal for a company scaling inventory and receivables at 60% growth. TTM operating cash flow is $83.2B, FCF $77.3B. That is not a warning sign; it is the working capital needed to feed hyper-growth.

**Bear point 5: No dividend support.**  
Correct — NVDA is a total-return story. The token $0.01 dividend is irrelevant. The buyback is the real capital return, and it is massive. If you want income, look elsewhere. If you want growth, NVDA is the leader.
