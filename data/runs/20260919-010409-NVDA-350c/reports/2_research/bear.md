## Competitive Weaknesses: The Moat Is Being Attacked

You describe CUDA as an unassailable moat. It’s strong, but it’s also the most obvious target in tech.

NVDA’s largest customers — hyperscalers — are designing their own silicon: Google TPU, Amazon Trainium/Inferentia, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA. AMD is pushing MI300/MI400. Intel has Gaudi. Chinese players are being forced to develop domestic alternatives. The more NVDA charges, the more incentive its customers have to diversify away. That is a classic innovator’s dilemma: your best customers become your competitors.

CUDA lock-in is real, but open-source frameworks, PyTorch, ROCm, and customer pressure are all eroding the absolute dependence over time. The bull case assumes NVDA keeps 90%+ share and 73%+ gross margins indefinitely. That’s a heroic assumption in a cyclical, capital-intensive, policy-sensitive industry.
