
Bull Analyst: **Bull Case for MSFT (NMS): Buy the Coil, Own the AI Cash-Flow Inflection**

If you’re bearish on MSFT here, your case almost certainly rests on three things: capex is crushing free cash flow, gross margins are slipping, and the stock is expensive. I respect those points—but they’re either temporary, already reflected in the price, or misreading the investment cycle. Let me take that bear case head-on.

First, the growth story is not slowing. MSFT just delivered FY2026 revenue of **$331.84B, up 17.8% YoY**—the fastest growth in the four-year window shown. Q4 FY2026 revenue hit a record **$90.0B, up 17.7% YoY**, and sequential revenue growth actually accelerated through the year: +4.6%, +2.0%, then **+8.6%** in the June quarter. Operating income rose 20.8% to **$155.2B**, net income jumped 31.3% to **$133.7B**, and diluted EPS grew 31.6% to **$17.95**. Even if you strip out the ~$5.9B of investment gains the bear will flag, adjusted EPS is still around **$17.16**, up roughly 26% YoY. That is elite growth at massive scale.

Now the capex bear argument. Yes, capex exploded to **$115.9B, +79.6% YoY**, and that pushed free cash flow down 6.5% to **$67.0B**. But here’s what the bear misses: this is not a company in distress—it’s a company building the infrastructure for the next decade of AI and cloud demand. Operating cash flow still surged **34.4% to $182.9B**. Net PPE rose **46.8% to $337.3B**, and gross PPE is now **$455.9B**. That is capacity coming online. Deferred revenue—contracted future revenue—grew **12.6% to $75.7B**. And the balance sheet is bulletproof: net debt/EBITDA is **0.10x**, interest coverage is **55x**, ROE is **34%**, and ROIC is **~31%**. MSFT can fund this capex, pay **$26.4B** in dividends, buy back **$22.3B** of stock, and still end the year with **$76.7B** in cash and short-term investments. The bear is treating growth investment as a permanent expense. If capex merely flattens in FY2027, free cash flow inflects hard.

On gross margins, the bear points to four straight quarterly declines—69.0% to 67.2%. Fair. But MSFT still has a **67.9% gross margin** and expanded operating margin by **120bp to 46.8%** because opex leverage more than offset the mix shift toward cloud/AI infrastructure. Rising D&A is a real headwind, but it’s non-cash and tied to revenue-generating assets. As those data centers scale, utilization improves. A 67%+ gross margin with 46%+ operating margins is not a broken business model.

Valuation? At roughly **$498**, MSFT trades near **27.6x trailing EPS** and about **1.8% FCF yield**—but that FCF yield is artificially depressed by the capex supercycle. On operating cash flow, the yield is closer to **5.0%**. On EV/EBITDA, it’s around **17.8x**. For a company growing revenue 17.8%, EPS 31.6%, and ROIC over 30%, that is not a bubble. The bear’s valuation case requires ignoring the AI capacity being built today.

Insider selling? The CEO and CFO sold stock in September, but it was immediately after RSU/option vesting and fits the same annual 10b5-1 pattern as September 2025. The only meaningful insider buy was a director purchase at **$397** in February—and yes, that’s below today’s price. But insiders are not market timers, and they still hold enormous equity. That’s not a fundamental sell signal.

Technically, MSFT is coiled, not broken. The last close was **$497.93**, sitting right on its 10 EMA (**$497.92**) and VWMA (**$497.18**). Price is above the rising 50 SMA (**$473.09**) and the 200 SMA (**$430.47**) in a classic bullish alignment. The 50 SMA has climbed from **$397** in late July to **$473** now. MACD is below its signal, but still positive at **5.85**; RSI is neutral at **54.46**; ATR has compressed from ~17 to **10.97**. That is a textbook energy-storing consolidation. The band edges are clear: **$487** support, **$512** resistance. A volume-backed close above **$512** opens the **$517.78** cycle high and then continuation. A close below **$487** would be the bear’s first real victory—but the 50 SMA at **$473** is the deeper trend-defining support.

So here’s my bull recommendation for **MSFT**: accumulate on pullbacks into **$487–490**, add aggressively on a volume-backed break above **$512**, with an initial target of **$517.78** and a strategic target of **$550+** as the AI capex cycle matures. Use **$473** as the line in the sand for the medium-term trend. The bear is fighting last quarter’s FCF print; the bull is positioning for the next AI-driven cash-flow cycle. MSFT has the growth, the moat, the balance sheet, and the technical setup to make the bull case the stronger one.
