By the time the 50 SMA crossed above the 200 SMA, most of the move was already in the price. The 200 SMA inflection is also very shallow — it went from 429.26 to 429.65, barely a rounding error. So the trader’s plan to add at 481 with a stop at 474 is not aligned with the trend timeframe. You would get shaken out on a routine correction, then watch MSFT resume higher without you. It resolves in a violent move, but not necessarily upward. The aggressive analyst says “after a golden cross and rising 200 SMA, the resolution is usually violent and upward.” That is an assertion, not a statistical fact. In reality, compression after a mature rally can just as easily mark a top. And given the negative momentum divergence and the fact that MSFT is 9.4% above its 50 SMA, the downside resolution is a real threat. The conservative approach is to wait for the break to happen and then participate with confirmation, not to front-run it with adds.

For a conservative mandate, that is the risk we must protect against. Missing a 12% gap up is an opportunity cost. Suffering a 12% gap down is a permanent capital impairment. Those are not symmetric. The firm’s objective is steady, reliable growth, not maximum upside. So we cannot treat those two outcomes as equivalent.

If price stays below VWMA, those buyers may become sellers on any bounce, creating resistance. The 10 EMA at 496.37 is also above price, confirming that the short-term trend is down. So the path of least resistance is down until proven otherwise.
